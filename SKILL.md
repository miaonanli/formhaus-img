# formhaus-img

Transform an uploaded photograph into a minimal Bauhaus-inspired editorial poster.

## Core composition

Create one vertical poster with two sections:

1. **Top: original photograph**
2. **Bottom: Bauhaus interpretation**

### Top photograph
- Place the uploaded photograph at the very top.
- The photo must touch the **top, left, and right edges** of the canvas.
- **Absolutely no padding, margin, border, frame, beige space, or white space around the top photo.**
- Do not put the photo inside a card or container.
- Preserve the original composition and recognizable content.
- Crop only when necessary.
- Only subtle editorial color correction is allowed.

### Bottom interpretation
Use a warm ivory/off-white paper field below the photo.

Analyze the image and identify:
- primary subject
- strongest silhouette
- dominant architectural or natural forms
- important spatial relationships
- distinctive lines, angles, curves, repetitions, and proportions

Reduce these into a small vocabulary of:
- circles and semicircles
- rectangles
- triangles
- straight lines and simple curves
- overlapping planes
- strong silhouettes
- basic geometric blocks

Do not simply trace or vectorize the photograph. Remove visual clutter and unnecessary detail. The result should feel designed rather than traced.

### Scale and negative space
- The Bauhaus illustration should occupy approximately **70% of the lower section width**.
- Do not stretch it edge-to-edge.
- Center it horizontally.
- Keep it compact.
- Surround it with generous ivory negative space.

### Color
Extract approximately 3–5 colors from the photograph:
- one dominant color
- one secondary color
- one dark structural color
- one light neutral
- optionally one accent

Keep colors slightly muted, warm, archival, and print-like. Classic Bauhaus red, blue, yellow, black, and off-white may be introduced only when they complement the source image.

### Style
Aim for early modernist visual communication, Bauhaus print design, geometric abstraction, architectural studies, and experimental European editorial graphics.

Use flat color fields, simple overlapping forms, and subtle paper/screen-print texture.

Avoid:
- gradients
- glossy effects
- photorealistic shading
- 3D rendering
- watercolor
- cartoon styling
- generic corporate vector illustration
- excessive detail

### Caption
Generate a unique English title based on the uploaded photograph.
- maximum 3 words
- preferably 1–2 words
- uppercase
- small geometric sans-serif
- generous letter spacing
- centered beneath the illustration

Do not reuse generic titles from previous images.

## Final rule

**TOP PHOTO = FULL BLEED, ABSOLUTELY NO PADDING.**  
**BOTTOM ILLUSTRATION = APPROXIMATELY 70% WIDTH, CENTERED.**  
**LOWER SECTION = GENEROUS NEGATIVE SPACE.**  
**SIMPLIFY AGGRESSIVELY. KEEP LESS. EMPHASIZE MORE.**
