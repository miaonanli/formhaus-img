# Reusable Prompt

Transform the uploaded photograph into a minimal Bauhaus-inspired editorial poster.

Create one vertical poster composed of two sections: the original photograph on top and a highly simplified Bauhaus interpretation below.

## TOP — ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH

Place the uploaded photograph at the very top of the poster.

IMPORTANT:
- The photograph must touch the TOP, LEFT, and RIGHT edges of the canvas.
- There must be absolutely NO padding, margin, border, frame, beige space, or white space around the top photograph.
- Do not place the photograph inside a card or container.
- Preserve the original photograph’s composition and recognizable content.
- Crop only when necessary to fit the poster composition.
- Do not redraw, stylize, or significantly alter the photograph.
- Only subtle editorial color correction is allowed.

The photograph should feel full-bleed and visually dominant.

## BOTTOM — BAUHAUS INTERPRETATION

Below the photograph, create a warm ivory / off-white paper field.

Reinterpret the photograph as an extremely minimal Bauhaus-inspired graphic composition.

Do NOT simply trace or vectorize the photograph.

Instead, analyze the image and identify:
- the primary subject
- the strongest silhouette
- the dominant architectural or natural forms
- important spatial relationships
- distinctive lines, angles, curves, repetitions, or proportions

Then reduce these elements into a small vocabulary of geometric forms.

Use circles, semicircles, rectangles, triangles, straight lines, simple curves, overlapping planes, strong silhouettes, and basic geometric blocks.

Complex objects should become simple visual symbols. Remove unnecessary background information, tiny details, textures, signs, clutter, and decorative elements.

The result should feel designed rather than traced.

## COMPOSITION

The Bauhaus illustration should occupy approximately 70% of the available width of the lower section.

Do NOT stretch the illustration edge-to-edge.

Center it horizontally and surround it with generous negative space. The illustration itself should remain relatively compact.

The large amount of empty ivory space is intentional.

Hierarchy:

FULL-BLEED PHOTOGRAPH  
↓  
LARGE QUIET SPACE  
↓  
SMALLER ABSTRACT BAUHAUS INTERPRETATION  
↓  
TITLE

## COLOR

Extract a limited palette of approximately 3–5 colors from the uploaded photograph.

Prioritize one dominant color, one secondary color, one dark structural color, one light neutral, and optionally one accent.

Colors should feel slightly muted, warm, archival, and print-like.

Classic Bauhaus red, blue, yellow, black, and off-white may be introduced only when they complement the original photograph. Do not force a standard palette onto every image.

Avoid gradients, glossy effects, photorealistic shading, 3D rendering, complex lighting, watercolor, cartoon styling, generic corporate vector illustration, and excessive detail.

## STYLE

The lower image should feel influenced by early modernist visual communication, Bauhaus print design, geometric abstraction, architectural studies, and experimental European editorial graphics.

Keep the geometry slightly human and imperfect rather than digitally sterile. A subtle paper or screen-print texture may be present.

## CAPTION

Generate a short English title based specifically on the uploaded photograph.

Maximum 3 words; preferably 1–2 words. Use uppercase letters, a very small clean geometric sans-serif, and generous letter spacing. Center it beneath the Bauhaus illustration.

Do not reuse generic titles from previous images.

## FINAL DIRECTION

The upper photograph documents reality. The lower composition reduces that reality to its essential visual grammar.

Do not decorate the photograph. Do not reproduce every object. Do not overfill the lower section.

Find the few forms that make the photograph recognizable and exaggerate their visual relationships.

Most importantly:

TOP PHOTO = FULL BLEED, ABSOLUTELY NO PADDING.
BOTTOM ILLUSTRATION = APPROXIMATELY 70% WIDTH, CENTERED.
LOWER SECTION = GENEROUS NEGATIVE SPACE.
SIMPLIFY AGGRESSIVELY.
KEEP LESS. EMPHASIZE MORE.

Generate the finished poster directly.
